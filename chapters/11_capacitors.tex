Electrochemical double-layer capacitors are a class of energy storage devices characterized by high power and low energy density. The process of storing energy is based on adsorption of ions, a non-Faradic process, which gives the characteristic fast kinetics at the expense of capacity; they also exhibit high self discharge. The capacity is due to the electrode/electrolyte surface area, therefore materials with high porosity are particularly indicated. In this section, it is reported a work on self standing activated carbon electrodes in three-electrode cell. The presence of the reference electrode allows to separate the time-varying impedance of positive and negative electrodes during the operation of the device.
The electrodes where produced by Dr. Nicolò Pianta in the group of Prof. Riccardo Ruffo at the University of Milano-Bicocca. He brought the electrodes in Bremen during his visiting period where we worked in close collaboration on the establishments of the measurement apparatus and software. \subsection{Reference electrode placement}
% Two possible positions:
% central (between the two carbon electrodes)
% side (immersed in electrolyte reservoir above the stack)

Two possible positions of the reference electrode were evaluated: (i) the ``side position,'' where the reference is placed above or to the side of the active stack in a flooded region of the cell; (ii) the ``central position,'' where the Pb wire is inserted in the middle, directly between the two carbon electrodes. As highlighted in Ref.~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}, placing the reference on the side often leads to mid/high-frequency artifacts, caused by non-uniform current paths and additional electrolyte resistances. A direct comparison of impedance spectra from the two positions showed that a loop-like artifact appears if the reference is displaced too far from the working electrodes. Consequently, for the DEIS experiments presented here, the reference was placed in the central region of the stack, minimizing parasitic inductive/capacitive pathways.

\subsection{Multisine excitation}

We generated a multi-sine waveform covering frequencies from about 0.1\,Hz up to 100\,kHz, with 6--8 points per decade. Frequencies were constrained to avoid intermodulation (i.e., every sine component was chosen so that its frequency is not a simple linear combination of at least four others). Each sine amplitude was estimated by preliminary EIS tests to guarantee sufficiently large signals above the measurement noise, while avoiding strong nonlinearity. The phase of each sine was randomized to minimize the crest factor.

\subsection{Impedance estimation}
% DMFA parameters:
% -bandwidth (e.g., 0.01-0.05 Hz)
% -n = 8
% -window length and overlap
% Lowest frequency discarded due to zero-frequency skirt

From the time-sampled voltage and current, we computed the time-resolved impedance via Dynamic Multi-Frequency Analysis (DMFA)~\cite{Koster2017,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}. This procedure applies narrow band-pass filtering in the frequency domain, isolating each sine component from the high-frequency carrier and from the low-frequency drift. By repeating the filtering over a sliding window of about 10\,s, we obtain $\mathbf{Z}(t,f)$. Simultaneously, we reconstruct the CV-like current--voltage profiles at 1\,s resolution by applying a zero-frequency filter around the drift. Details on the python-based DMFA open-source implementation are reported in Ref.~\cite{chukwuStatisticalAnalysisMeasurement2022} and references therein.

\section{Physical model}
% No Faradaic reactions
% Impedance ≈ ideal capacitor + distributed resistance
% But real porous materials show:
% - distributed RC elements
% - finite-length diffusion of ions within pores
% - frequency-dependent capacitance (constant-phase element behaviour)

The non-Faradaic adsorption mechanism for EDLC electrodes can be pictured as parallel pathways of double-layer charging and pore adsorption, each limited by finite or semi-infinite transport. A known approach~\cite{Janes2007,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a} uses a circuit containing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_s$: Ohmic series resistance from electrolyte/conductors,
    \item $C_{\text{dl}}$ or CPE: the double-layer capacitor (or constant-phase element) across the micropore interface,
    \item $C_{\text{ad}}$ (or Warburg branch): modeling slow adsorption phenomena or diffusion-limited infiltration,
    \item possibly a Finite-Length Warburg (FLW) for the negative electrode, capturing localized diffusion.
\end{itemize}
The exact best-fit topology can differ for each electrode, indicating that identical carbon membranes under different polarization can exhibit distinct distributed resistances and pore-filling characteristics.

Despite the nominally identical activated carbon electrodes, the positive side consistently shows a stronger frequency dispersion and larger apparent capacitance, likely due to local oxygen-containing species adsorbed upon overcharging. Conversely, the negative electrodes impedance shape more closely resembles an ideal double-layer: a single semicircle or depressed arc merges into a near-vertical line at low frequencies. Similar discrepancies appear in Refs.~\cite{ppiantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023aTribbia2020}, confirming that potential-dependent side reactions or ionic rearrangements can occur in symmetrical carbon electrodes at different operating windows.

From the reconstructed current--voltage curves, we estimated the average electrode capacitance with
\[
C_{\mathrm{avg}} \;=\; \frac{i_{\mathrm{drift}}}{\,dV/dt\,},
\]
where $i_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is the net current from the potential sweep. We also extracted $C_\mathrm{dl}$, $C_\mathrm{ad}$, and any Warburg-like terms from equivalent circuit regressions. Both methods yield values of about 40--50\,F\,g$^{-1}$ for each electrode, consistent with free-standing activated carbons in alkaline media~\cite{Tribbia2020}. The positive electrode, however, shows a noticeable rise in adsorption-related capacitance above 0.9\,V vs.\ RHE, highlighting contributions from surface-active species formed near the oxygen-evolution threshold~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}.

\section{Remarks and perspective}
In this chapter, we demonstrated the application of non-stationary impedance spectroscopy to an adsorption-controlled energy storage system. Despite using chemically identical carbon electrodes, the three-electrode DEIS reveals distinct shapes and time constants at the positive and negative electrodes, driven by local potential, electrode history, and side reactions. Our results are consistent with prior literature~\cite{Tribbia2020,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}, and confirm the methodological advantages of DEIS over classical EIS in disentangling dynamic adsorption phenomena and electrode asymmetry. Future work will leverage the same approach in galvanostatic drifts, or in synergy with in-situ spectroscopic probes, to explore aging mechanisms and microscopic adsorption processes.
