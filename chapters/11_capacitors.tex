\chapter{Non-Faradaic process in symmetric cell}
\label{chap:capacitor}

Electrochemical double-layer capacitors are a class of energy storage devices characterized by high power and low energy density. The process of storing energy is based on adsorption of ions, a non-Faradaic process, which gives the characteristic fast kinetics at the expense of capacity; they also exhibit high self-discharge. The capacity depends on the electrode-electrolyte surface area, so materials with high porosity are particularly desirable. This section reports work on self-standing activated carbon electrodes in a three-electrode cell. The presence of the reference electrode allows separation of the instantaneous impedance of positive and negative electrodes during device operation.
In a dynamic impedance spectroscopy measurement, the multi-sine was superimposed onto a cyclic voltammetry drift.

The results of this project led to a publication \cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}, and the data are available in a public repository\sidenote{doi: 10.6084/m9.figshare.21082168}.

\section{Experimental section}
\subsection{Materials and cell assembly}
\subsubsection{Electrode material}
The freestanding activated carbon electrodes were prepared by Dr. Nicolò Pianta (University of Milano-Bicocca) according to reference \cite{tribbiaSuperCap2020} to ensure a binder-free, highly porous network with large accessible surface area. The synthesis route includes carbonization and physical activation of a precursor to achieve high microporosity.
Disks of 12\,mm diameter (approximately 1.54\,cm$^2$ projected area) were punched from the freestanding membranes. Each disk weighed approximately 23.5\,mg of active carbon. The high surface area allows for capacitance in the range of tens of F\,g$^{-1}$.

\subsubsection{Electrolyte}
A 10\,mol\,kg$^{-1}$ KF aqueous solution was selected as the electrolyte. Compared to neutral dilute solutions, this composition (pH~10.7) offers a wider potential window and a relatively stable interface. The device stores energy mainly by the adsorption of K$^+$ and F$^-$ species at the electrode double layer~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}.

The electrodes were introduced into a T-shaped PEEK cell body, with two stainless-steel pistons acting as current collectors and applying mild compression to ensure low interfacial resistance. Two glass-fiber disks served as separators, thoroughly soaked in the KF solution. A key challenge was the choice and placement of the reference electrode within this compact geometry. Standard Ag/AgCl probes prove bulky and often introduce junction potential errors at high pH. Thus, we adopted a Pb/PbF$_2$ electrode~\cite{pastaLeadFlouride2012,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a} by inserting a thin Pb wire inside the stack, where a thin layer of PbF$_2$ formed \textit{in situ}, yielding a stable potential around 191\,mV vs.\ RHE.

Two possible positions of the reference electrode were evaluated: the "side position", where the reference is placed above or to the side of the active stack in a flooded region of the cell; and the "central position", where the Pb wire is inserted in the middle, directly between the two carbon electrodes.
They are schematically illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:edlc_ref_position}.
As highlighted in reference~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}, placing the reference on the side often leads to mid- and high-frequency artifacts caused by non-uniform current paths and additional electrolyte resistances. A direct comparison of impedance spectra from the two positions showed that a loop-like artifact appears if the reference is displaced too far from the working electrodes. Consequently, for the DEIS experiments presented here, the reference was placed in the central region of the stack, minimizing parasitic inductive and capacitive pathways.


\subsection{DEIS measurement}

In this work, we aimed to measure simultaneously the impedance of both cell electrodes during operation.
Since each auxiliary port of the potentiostat channel has only the working electrode voltage, we needed to use a second channel for the acquisition of the full cell.
One channel was connected to the working and counter electrodes of the cell, short-circuiting the reference and counter sensing connectors.
This provides the cyclic voltammetry perturbation to the cell where the voltage sweep is applied between working and counter electrode, simulating the operation of the device.
A second channel was set to open-circuit mode, with its working and reference sensing connected to the working and reference electrodes of the cell for the recording of the working electrode half-cell voltage drop.
The current flows equally in series connection while the voltage drop of the counter half-cell is obtained by subtraction of the total cell voltage from the working half-cell voltage.

Figure \ref{fig:reference-electrode-impedance} illustrates the role of the reference electrode in separating the working electrode response from counter-electrode and electrolyte contributions, which is essential for reliable impedance measurements.

\subsubsection{Multi-sine excitation}

We generated a multi-sine waveform covering frequencies from about 0.1 Hz up to 100 kHz, with 8 points per decade. Frequencies were constrained to avoid intermodulation. Each sine amplitude was estimated by preliminary EIS tests to guarantee sufficiently large signals above the measurement noise, while avoiding strong nonlinearity. The phase of each sine was randomized to minimize the crest factor.

\subsubsection{Applied drift}
 
A quasi-linear potential sweep of 8\,mV\,s$^{-1}$ was superimposed onto the multi-sine. Over one full cycle (charge then discharge) the cell spans from near 0\,V (completely discharged) to $\approx1.2$\,V (fully charged) and back, requiring about 250\,s. This scanning is repeated for a few consecutive cycles. Because the drift period is 250\,s, we restrict ourselves to multi-sine frequencies of 0.3\,Hz and higher in the final analysis, to minimize overlap with the drift fundamental frequency~\cite{battistelPhysicalDefinitionDynamic2019}.

\subsubsection{Impedance estimation}

From the multi-frequency voltage and current, we estimated the time-resolved impedance via DMFA \cite{kosterDynamicImpedanceSpectroscopy2017a,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}. Simultaneously, we reconstructed the CV-like current--voltage profiles at 1\,s resolution by applying a zero-frequency filter around the drift. 

\subsection{Physical model}

The non-Faradaic adsorption mechanism for EDLC electrodes can be pictured as parallel pathways of double-layer charging and pore adsorption, each limited by finite or semi-infinite transport \cite{janesCharacterizationActivatedNanoporous2007}. 
The model is represented as the equivalent circuit model in Figure \ref{fig:adsorbtion_model_circuit}

\subsection{Qualitative evaluation of the impedance}

The DEIS data show both electrodes presenting predominantly capacitive spectra under moderate voltages (below $\approx0.7$\,V vs.\ RHE). However, near the upper region ($\approx1.2$\,V vs.\ RHE at the positive electrode), an additional low-frequency arc develops, signifying parasitic side reactions (likely oxygen evolution).
Figure \ref{fig:edlc_deis_timefreq} illustrates the time evolution of the impedance modulus at representative mid-frequency (10 Hz) and low-frequency (300 mHz) components for both electrodes.
The negative electrode impedance remains relatively stable, while the positive electrode experiences a marked drift in the low-frequency domain once the potential exceeds $\sim1.0$\,V vs.\ RHE.

\begin{widefigure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width = \linewidth]{figures/chap_supercap/deis_all_electrode_v2.png}
    \caption{Time evolution of selected impedance components during CV.
    Modulus of impedance at 10 Hz and 300 mHz for positive and negative activated carbon electrodes during cyclic voltammetry, highlighting the onset of low-frequency drift at the positive electrode near the upper potential limit. Reproduced from \cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}.}
    \label{fig:edlc_deis_timefreq}
\end{widefigure}

Despite the nominally identical activated carbon electrodes, the positive side consistently shows stronger frequency dispersion and larger apparent capacitance, likely due to local oxygen-containing species adsorbed upon overcharging.
Conversely, the negative electrode's impedance shape more closely resembles an ideal double-layer: a single semicircle or depressed arc merges into a near-vertical line at low frequencies. Similar discrepancies appear in References~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a,tribbiaSuperCap2020}, confirming that potential-dependent side reactions or ionic rearrangements can occur in symmetrical carbon electrodes at different operating windows.

\subsection{Capacitance estimation}

From the reconstructed current-voltage curves, we estimated the average electrode capacitance with
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{avg}} \;=\; \frac{i_{\mathrm{drift}}}{\,dV/dt\,},
\end{equation}
where $i_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is the net current from the potential sweep. We also extracted $C_\mathrm{dl}$, $C_\mathrm{ad}$, and any Warburg-like terms from equivalent circuit regressions. Both methods yield values of approximately 40--50\,F\,g$^{-1}$ for each electrode, consistent with free-standing activated carbons in alkaline media~\cite{tribbiaSuperCap2020}. The positive electrode, however, shows a noticeable rise in adsorption-related capacitance above 0.9\,V vs.\ RHE, highlighting contributions from surface-active species formed near the oxygen-evolution threshold~\cite{piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}.

For electrochemical capacitor the capacitance is a key performance metric. For porous electrodes the impedance if fitted with the CPE elements and the effective capacitance $C_{eff}$ can be calculated with the formula \cite{HIRSCHORN20106218} 
\begin{equation}
    C_{eff} = Q_{dl}^{\frac{1}{n}} R_{ad}^{\frac{1-n}{n}},
\end{equation}
where $Q_{dl}$ and $n$ are the CPE parameters and $R_{ad}$ the adsorbtion capacitance.
Figure \ref{fig:capacitance estimation} shows the values for the CPE capacitance $Q_{dl}$  estimated via equivalent circuit fitting and the calculated effective capacitance $C_{eff}$ for positive and negative electrodes as a function of the potential.
The effective capacitance, in contrast to the CPE capacitance, reflects properly the reversibility of the adsorption process in EDLCs.

\section{Summary and conclusions}
In this chapter, we described the simultaneous measurement of dynamic impedance spectroscopy of both half-cells for a symmetric super-capacitor based on activated nano-porous carbon electrodes.
The experiment highlights that even chemically identical electrodes can have different interfacial impedance, something not apparent from direct current methods.
Other than manufacturing variation, the two electrodes have different history. Analyzing such systems with the whole cell impedance doesn't inform on the specific reactions. This is important to consider for more complex systems like batteries were electrode processes might be hidden when looking at the whole cell response.

% In this chapter, we demonstrated the application of non-stationary impedance spectroscopy to an adsorption-controlled energy storage system. Despite using chemically identical carbon electrodes, the three-electrode DEIS reveals distinct shapes and time constants at the positive and negative electrodes, driven by local potential, electrode history, and side reactions. Our results are consistent with prior literature~\cite{tribbiaSuperCap2020,piantaEvaluationKineticParameters2023a}, and confirm the methodological advantages of DEIS over classical EIS in disentangling dynamic adsorption phenomena and electrode asymmetry. Future work will leverage the same approach in galvanostatic drifts, or in synergy with in-situ spectroscopic probes, to explore aging mechanisms and microscopic adsorption processes.
