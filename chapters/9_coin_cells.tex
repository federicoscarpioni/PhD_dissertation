In this chapter, I present the measurement of dynamic impedance of batteries during operation. The aim is to demonstrate the advantages of impedance measurements in non-stationary condition compared to the classical stationary approach.

I acquired a set of identical commercial coin cells and tested them with dynamic impednace method during cycling and normal electrochemical measurement. The results from the dynamic impedance experiment are compared with a sereis of classic stationary impedance spectrscopy measurements. 

Furthermore I prove that the presence of a multisine perturbation, continously apply during the dynamic impedance spectroscopy test,  doesn't effect the aging of the cells.

\section{Experimental set-up}

\subsection{Cell choice}

For this research, I acquired a set of identical Panasonic ML2020 coin cell batteries containing manganese dioxide (MnO2) as positive electrode and lithium-aluminum alloy as negative electrode. The lithium content is approximately 0.02g. These cells utilize an electrolyte solution composed of 1,2-dimethoxyethane and ethylene glycol dimethyl ether (EGDME) combined with an unspecified lithium salt compound.

According to manufacturer specifications, these batteries have a nominal capacity of 45mAh and are designed to operate within a voltage window of 1-3V, with a recommended charging current of 100$\mu$A. 

The cells exhibit relatively slow kinetics compared to NMC/graphite systems, due to the viscosity of the electrolyte, and rapid capacity fade—characteristics already at modest c-rates. In fact, they are intended as buffer for low-power electronics with reduced self-discharge.

The limitations are actually favorable for rapidly iterate optimizatio of the set-up and the use of commercial cells ensure reporducibility  for the validation of the method. 

\subsection{Dynamic impedance spectoscopy measurement}
I conducted the measurements of this chapter using a setup configured for broadband multi-sine waves: two channels of the generator apply two waveforms for a total of 7 decades of perturbation, and the computer processes the streamed data from the scope in real time as described in section \ref{On-line computation}. Three of the six channels of the BioLogic SP300 electrochemical workstation measure simultaneously, exploiting the multi-threading capability of the software \emph{DEIStools}. The connection of the insturments and the software is the one described in the Methods chapter. The dynamic impedance spetrscopy experiment contains a total of 9 cells.

THe mutli-sine waveform excitation contains 49 frequencies components in the range 10mHz - 100kHz, generated at 250 kSa/s. The total waveform is splitted in two parts as described in section XXX as a workaround for the memory limitation of one channel of the generator. The amplitude of the multi-sine components is empiracal. Firstly I measured a complete cycle of the battery with dynamic impedance spectroscopy using a equal amplitudes on a test cell (not part of the dataset), and then I used the avarage of the module of such impednace and used it to calculate the amplitudes for the input in order to obtain an output with equal magnitude for each frequencies. In mathematical therms, being $|Z_{test}(\omega)|_{avg}$ the avarege of the impedance module, the desired output voltage is unitary for all frequencies, enche the current amplitude follows Ohm's law as:

$$
i(\omega_k) = \frac{1}{|Z_{test}(\omega_k)|_{avg}}.
$$

The output of the waveform generator is a voltage and it is converted to current by the electrochemical workstation during the chronoamperometry technique using the active measurement resistor ($R_m$). For this experiments I choose always the same current range of 10 mA for the sequence that translates to a measurement resistor of 100 Ohms. Hence for the galvanostatic step the actual peak-to-peak voltage of the generated waveform is 

$$
V(\omega_k) = R_m \times i(\omega_k).
$$

For the potentiostatic step instead the amplitude of the multisine is 

$$
V(\omega_k) = |Z_{test}(\omega_k)|_{avg}.
$$

The electrochemical workstation applies a constant current - constant voltage sequence with higher voltage limit of 3V in charge, and same value for the constant voltage step with a limit on the minium current of 1 mA. The lower voltage limit for discharge was 1V. The current applied was 4.5 mA equivalent to a c-rate of C/10. Becasue of the presence of the multisine, the limits are checked during the online computation algorithm as avarage value of the block of data (as described in section XXX). The cell are connected as new and the measurements run for 30 cycles continously until the cell reaches the end of life. 

To allow the update of the waveform in the set-up between galvanostatic and potentiostatic techniques in the sequence, a 3 second open sircuit is added in between. 

The voltage and current signals are sampled with a two-channel PicoScope 4000 series with a sampling rate of 500 kSa/s.

After the experiment the automated software returns the impedance of the high frequencies 10Hz-100kHz computed online with the short-time FFT method and the voltage and current signals form the cells at the reduced sampling rate of 50 Sa/s. The signals contains the lowest 21 frequencies components from 10mHz to around 8 Hz.

\subsection{Classic electrochemical test}

I performed cycling experiments with the same constant current - constant voltage protocol in BioLogic battery cycler on 12 cells from new to aged with the aim to compare the degradation in presence of the multisine excitation and without.

I also prepared an experiment performing a classic electrochemial impednace spectroscopy technique in the same battery cycler. I prepared a sequence to interrupt the cycling protocol every 10 per cent of the nominal capacity and included a rest of 1 hour before the measurement of the impedance in the frequency range 10mHZ-20kHz (8 points per decade).

\subsection{The effect of the multisine on the aging}

I verified that the constant superimposition of a multisine to the drift does not induce any alteration of the aging behaviour. Figure \ref{fig:aging_comp} shows the statistical summary discharge capacity for 12 cells cycled with DC signal and 9 cell cycled with the mutli-sine superimposed. No significal difference is noticible.

\begin{figure} [H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width = \textwidth]{figures/results_coin/Boxplot_aging.png}
  \caption{svg image}
  \label{fig:aging_comp}
\end{figure}

For this experiment the cells are small and cycled with slow c-rate. To peak to peak amplitude of the multisine is also of a similir value. It is important that the excitation doesn't impact the performance of the batteries. This might not be the case for bigger batteries and higher c-rates.
