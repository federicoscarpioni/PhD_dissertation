\chapter{Coin cells: characterization at the cell level}
\label{chap:coins}

% Objectives
In this chapter, it is presented the measurement of dynamic impedance on commercial batteries during operation. 
The aim is to demonstrate the applicability on batteries of the mixed method for instantaous impedance estimation in non-stationary conditions.

% Why coin cells
I choose to make this experiment on commercial coin cells of theretical capacity of 45mAh, because of the conveniancy of buy big lots, for cheap. 
I acquired a set of identical commercial coin cells and tested them with dynamic impednace method during cycling and conventional electrochemical measurement as comparison. 
The results from the dynamic impedance experiment are compared with a sereis of classic stationary impedance spectrscopy measurements. 

Furthermore I prove that the presence of a multisine perturbation, continously applyed during the dynamic impedance spectroscopy test,  doesn't effect the aging of the cells.

\section{Experimental set-up}

\subsection{Cell choice}
% Cell chemistry and implications
For this research, I acquired a set of identical Panasonic ML2020 coin cell batteries containing manganese dioxide (MnO2) as positive electrode and lithium-aluminum alloy as negative electrode. 
The lithium content is approximately 0.02g. 
These cells utilize an electrolyte solution composed of 1,2-dimethoxyethane and ethylene glycol dimethyl ether (EGDME) combined with an unspecified lithium salt compound.

According to manufacturer specifications, these batteries have a nominal capacity of 45mAh and are designed to operate within a voltage window of 1-3V, with a recommended charging current of 100$\mu$A. 

The cells exhibit relatively slow kinetics compared to NMC/graphite systems, due to the viscosity of the electrolyte, and rapid capacity fade—characteristics already at modest c-rates. 
They are designed as buffer for low-power electronics hence a viscous solvent is used as electrolyte to reduced  self-discharge.
When cycling at a C-rate of C/10 the cells degrade fast, in just circa 22 cycels, and short-circuits were observed. 
I supposed the lithium plating into dendrici form.

The limitations are actually favorable for rapidly iterate optimizations of the set-up.
The use of commercial cells ensures reporducibility for the validation of the method. 

\subsection{Dynamic impedance spectoscopy measurements}
\subsubsection{Setup description}
I conducted the measurements of this chapter using the setup configured for broadband multi-sine waves: two channels of the generator apply two multi-sine excitation for a total of 7 frequency decades, and the computer processes the streamed data from the scope in real time as described in section \ref{On-line computation}. 
Three of the six channels of the BioLogic SP300 potentiostat measured simultaneously thanks to the multi-threading capability of the software \emph{DEIStools}. 
The connection of the insturments is reported in Figure XXXX.
%[Add figure of the set-up ]

\subsubsection{Multi-sine design}
The mutli-sine waveform excitation contains 49 frequencies components in the range 10mHz - 100kHz, generated at 250 kSa/s, hence one period is 100s. 
The total waveform is splitted in two parts as described in Section \ref{sec:multisine split} as a workaround for the memory limitation of one channel of the generator. 
The amplitude of the mutli-sine was optimized from a previous experiment as described in Section \ref{sec:amplitude optim}. 

\subsubsection{Applied drift}
The potentiostat applies a constant current - constant voltage sequence with higher voltage limit of 3V in charge, and same value for the constant voltage step with a limit on the minium current of 1 mA. The lower voltage limit for discharge was 1V. The current applied was 4.5 mA equivalent to a c-rate of C/10. Becasue of the presence of the multisine, the limits are checked during the online computation algorithm as avarage value of the block of data (as described in section XXX). The cell are connected as new and the measurements run for 30 cycles continously until the cell reaches the end of life. 

To allow the update of the waveform in the set-up between galvanostatic and potentiostatic techniques in the sequence, a 3 second open sircuit is added in between. 

The voltage and current signals are sampled with a two-channel PicoScope 4000 series with a sampling rate of 500 kSa/s.

After the experiment the automated software returns the impedance of the high frequencies 10Hz-100kHz computed online with the short-time FFT method and the voltage and current signals form the cells at the reduced sampling rate of 50 Sa/s. The signals contains the lowest 21 frequencies components from 10mHz to around 8 Hz.

The impedance of the lower frequency band and the drift are estimated with the DMFA using a filter bandwidth of 0.01 Hz that leads to a time resolution of 100s, equal to one period of multisine and the signal block used in the online computation. \subsection{Classic electrochemical test}
I performed cycling experiments with the same constant current - constant voltage protocol in BioLogic battery cycler on 12 cells from new to aged with the aim to compare the degradation in presence of the multisine excitation and without.

\subsubsection{EIS during interrupted cycling}
I also prepared an experiment performing a classic electrochemial impednace spectroscopy technique in the same battery cycler. I prepared a sequence to interrupt the cycling protocol every 10 per cent of the nominal capacity and included a rest of 1 hour before the measurement of the impedance in the frequency range 10mHZ-20kHz (8 points per decade).
